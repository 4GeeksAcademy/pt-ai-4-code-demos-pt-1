Our Place in the Universe

Published September 8, 2026 · Updated today

The Solar System is the gravitationally bound system of the Sun and the objects that orbit it. It formed 4.6 billion years ago from the gravitational collapse of a giant interstellar molecular cloud.

"The Solar System is located in the Milky Way galaxy, about 27,000 light-years from the galactic center, on the inner edge of one of the spiral-shaped concentrations of gas and dust called the Orion Arm."

— Wikipedia

There are eight known planets orbiting the Sun, along with dwarf planets, moons, asteroids, comets, and other small bodies. The four inner planets are rocky, while the four outer planets are gas or ice giants.

Space Exploration Timeline

1957

Sputnik 1 — the first artificial satellite, launched by the Soviet Union.

1969

Apollo 11 — humans first set foot on the Moon. "That's one small step for man…"

1977

Voyager 2 launched — it later became the only spacecraft to visit Uranus and Neptune.

1990

The Hubble Space Telescope launched, revolutionising astronomy with breathtaking deep-space images.

2021

James Webb Space Telescope launched — the most powerful space telescope ever built.

🌠 Did You Know?

The Sun accounts for about 99.86% of the total mass of the Solar System. You could fit roughly 1.3 million Earths inside the Sun!

How do we know this?

Scientists calculate the Sun's mass by observing the orbital periods of the planets using Kepler's laws of planetary motion and Newton's law of universal gravitation.

📖 Glossary of Terms

Terrestrial Planet
A planet composed primarily of silicate rocks or metals. The four inner planets — Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars — are terrestrial.
Gas Giant
A large planet composed mostly of hydrogen and helium. Jupiter and Saturn are gas giants.
Ice Giant
A giant planet made of heavier volatile elements — water, methane, and ammonia — above a rocky core. Uranus and Neptune are ice giants.
Asteroid Belt
A region between Mars and Jupiter containing millions of rocky bodies, the largest being the dwarf planet Ceres.
